In the portal, a client can raise a ticket any time, follow what's happening on it, and reply - without an email getting lost on the way. Everything they do lands on the ticket in ITFlow, just as an email reply would.

## The tickets list

![The client's tickets](portal-tickets.webp "Tickets: what's open, what's waiting on them, and what's been fixed")

The tabs are where each ticket stands for the client: **Open**, **Waiting on you** (you've replied and need an answer), **Resolved** (fixed - for them to confirm), **Closed** and **All**. They can search by subject or number.

Most contacts see the tickets they raised. Primary and technical contacts see every ticket at their company, with **Everyone** and **Mine** to switch between the two.

## Raise a ticket

**New ticket** asks what they need. **Report a problem** is a short form:

- a **Subject**, in a few words
- **What is happening?** - what they were doing, any error message, and when it started
- **How much is it getting in the way?** - which sets how urgent it is
- **What is it about?** and **Which device?** - their own devices are listed
- **Screenshots or files**, dropped in or chosen

![Reporting a problem](portal-ticket-problem.webp "Report a problem: what's wrong, how much it's in the way, and which device")

**What happens next** alongside tells them what to expect, with your phone number for when it's stopping everyone.

Clients can also start a ticket from elsewhere in the portal: **Report a problem** on a device fills the device in, and **Ask us** on an invoice or quote fills in the subject.

### Request forms

Under **Or ask for something**, they see your request forms: "Ask for a new computer", "Onboard a new employee", "Someone is leaving", "Report a suspicious email". Each asks for exactly what you need to do the job - the new starter's name, start date, manager and whose access to copy - so you're not chasing the details afterwards.

![The choice of a problem or a request form](portal-ticket-new.webp "New ticket: report a problem, or ask for something with one of your forms")

![A request form for onboarding a new employee](portal-ticket-form.webp "A request form asks for exactly what you need to do the job")

The ticket is raised from the form's template, with its category, priority, tasks and who it's assigned to. Request forms are ticket templates offered in the portal - for every client or some, to everyone or only primary and technical contacts. See [Ticket templates](/admin/ticket-templates).

## Follow a ticket

![A ticket in the portal](portal-ticket.webp "A ticket: where it stands, the conversation, and the details")

The ticket shows where it stands - **Received**, **In progress**, **Resolved**, **Closed** - and the conversation, oldest first, with their own messages on the right. Your internal notes never show. Down the side: its status, priority, category, who raised it, who's working on it, the device and how many tasks are done.

- **Add a reply**, with files, and click **Send reply**.
- **It's fixed** marks it resolved themselves.
- When you've resolved it, they're asked to confirm: **Yes, close it**, or **No, reopen it** if it's still not right.
- Once it's closed, they can rate it **Good** or **Not good**. Ratings show in the Customer Satisfaction report.

### Approvals

When a task on a ticket needs the client's say-so - buying a replacement, giving someone admin access - the contact you picked sees it under **Waiting for your approval** on the ticket and on their home page, with **Approve**. They can approve it from the email too. The task is marked approved in ITFlow. See [Working a ticket](/service-desk/working-a-ticket#tasks).

## The ticket link in emails {#ticket-link}

Your ticket emails have a link to the ticket that opens without signing in - for whoever the email went to, contact or not. It looks like the portal's ticket page: your logo, where the ticket stands, and the conversation oldest first, with the files that came with each message. Alongside are who's working on it and on which device, and your phone, email and website.

![The ticket link in an email](portal-ticket-link.webp "The ticket link: where it stands, work booked, the conversation, and how to reply")

It also shows:

- when you'll reply, while the SLA's response time is still ahead
- work booked on the ticket - onsite, with the site's address, or remote - with **Add to calendar**
- tasks waiting for the client's approval, and who was emailed the link to approve them
- **Reply by email**, with the ticket number in the subject so the reply lands on the ticket, **Reply in the client portal**, and **Call** for an Urgent or High ticket

Replies come by email or from the portal, where you know who wrote them - the link can also go to people watching the ticket. Internal notes and their files never show. Closing, reopening and rating the ticket are buttons on the page, so nothing that follows links in an email can close or reopen a ticket by itself.

> [!TIP]
> Ticket emails carry the conversation too: the new message first, then the earlier public replies, newest first, and the request at the end - each as a short excerpt, with who wrote it and when. An email says what the ticket is about even when nothing new was written, such as Resolved or Work booked.
